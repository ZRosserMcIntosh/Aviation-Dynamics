# Validation: the generated jets

Phenom 300E, Citation CJ4, Learjet 45, CRJ700 and 737-800 are generated by
`tools/jetgen/jetgen.py` from short specifications of what is published
for each type, then calibrated by `tools/jetgen/calibrate.mjs` against the
few performance figures that pin a model down. The assertions are in
`tests/test_jetgen.cpp`; `node tools/jetgen/check.mjs` repeats the flight
checks in the web build.

These are **class-accurate, not type-certified**: each type's dimensions,
weights, engines, limits and systems are its own, but its aerodynamics are
the Top G's validated arrangement scaled to it. Treat any figure below that
is marked *predicted* as a sanity check, not a validation.

## Method

1. **Published inputs** (per type, cited in each JSON's `sources`): length,
   fuselage radius, span, wing area and quarter-chord sweep; MTOW, MLW,
   basic operating and zero-fuel weights; usable fuel; static thrust, bypass
   ratio and TSFC; Vmo/Mmo, VLE, flap detents and VFEs; winglets, T-tail,
   engine mounting, thrust reversers, speedbrake detents.
2. **Layout on the Top G** (`data/aircraft/top_g.json`): the wing planform
   family scaled in span and chord to the type's area, sweep Λ outboard and
   Λ − 6° inboard; the Top G's sections, flaps and spoiler layout; tail
   areas from its volume coefficients (V_H 0.688; V_V from the Top G's
   0.0577, raised for a fuselage longer than the wing can balance, see
   *Dutch roll* below); empty CG at
   27.5 % MAC, CG limits and main gear in % MAC; inertia scaled by mass and
   size; drag build-up scaled by wetted area, nacelle size and wing area;
   nacelle radius 0.79 m · √(T / 75.2 kN). The 737's engines hang under
   the wing at 29 % semispan, with gear legs long enough for 0.5 m of
   nacelle clearance.
3. **Systems from the template.** The Top G's `electrical` and
   `hydraulics` blocks come over whole (any field added to the template
   reaches every generated jet) and only the values that scale with the
   type are overridden: two engine-driven generators at the type's rating
   (Phenom 300E, CJ4 and Learjet 45 2 × 300 A starter-generators; CRJ700
   2 × 400 A and 737-800 2 × 600 A as the 28 V DC equivalent of their
   30 and 90 kVA IDGs), the single-alternator fallback at their sum, the
   battery (44, 44, 40, 43 and 48 Ah), the loads' currents (0.8, 0.8, 0.9,
   1.3 and 1.6 × the Top G's, same names), two 3,000 psi hydraulic systems
   each on one engine with the electric pump's current scaled with the
   generators (on A; on A and B for the 737). The CRJ700's third system
   and the 737's standby system are not modelled. Each is an *estimated*
   entry in the file's provenance.
4. **Calibration** in the web build (the same core):
   - **Engine lapse.** Mattingly's lapse coefficient, bisected so that the
     maximum level speed at full thrust, with the altitude held, is the
     published maximum cruise speed.
   - **Flap lift.** Where an approach speed is published with its weight,
     the flap's clmax increment ratio is bisected so the landing-flap stall
     speed is Vref / 1.23 (Part 25) or Vref / 1.3 (Part 23 commuter).

   The results are in `tools/jetgen/calibration.json`, and the generator
   bakes them in.

The Phenom 300E's Vref is a prediction: its flaps were not calibrated, and
the generated aircraft's landing-flap stall speed at MLW (17,042 lb) gives
1.3 VS = 105 KIAS against the published 104.

**Ground loop, found and fixed.** The first generation scaled every tyre
stiffness with mass. That left the nose tyre about 1.7 times as stiff, per
newton of load, as the mains (the Top G's own ratio). On the shorter
wheelbases of the business jets, the nose tyre's destabilising side force
overcame the mains below VR. Hands off, the Phenom, CJ4 and Learjet yawed
30–40° off the centreline near 110 kt, and the CRJ diverged at VR. The
generator now gives the nose tyre the mains' cornering stiffness per newton
of static load (mid CG at MTOW). The heading drift at 115 kt is now under 2°.
The test above fails with the old stiffness.

## Dutch roll and the yaw damper (October 2026)

Flown in the browser, the CRJ700 was "extremely unstable below 130–140
KTAS": its Dutch roll grew, and in Sport mode it diverged. Two causes, both
fixed:

- **A fin sized for the Top G.** The fin came from the Top G's volume
  coefficient V_V 0.0577, but the destabilising yawing moment of a fuselage
  (Munk) grows with its length and cross-section, not with the wing. The
  CRJ's long, slim fuselage on a small wing needed more fin. The generator
  now adds fin volume in proportion to the fuselage's yawing moment
  (length × radius²) per wing area × span, beyond the Top G's own:
  V_V = 0.0577 + 0.6 · max(105 · L · R² / (S · b) − 0.0304, 0). That gives
  CRJ700 0.082, 737-800 0.077, Learjet 45 0.078, Phenom 300E 0.070 and
  CJ4 0.067.
- **No yaw damper.** All five types (and the Top G) have one. The core now
  models a series yaw damper (`controls.yaw_damper`). It washes out the yaw
  rate (3 s) less the rate a coordinated turn at the present bank needs, so
  it does not fight turn entries. The rudder it commands is that rate times
  2 s, scaled with (160 KEAS / EAS)² (within 0.25–4) and limited to 30 % of
  the rudder. It is active in flight above 60 KIAS and switchable
  (`setYawDamper`).

Test: a rudder doublet on the approach (1.35 VS, landing flap, gear down)
and clean at 200 KIAS. With the damper, the late bank swing is under a
quarter of the early one; without it, the swing does not grow (the fin
alone damps it, or holds it neutral).

## Known limits

- **Aerodynamics are the Top G's, scaled.** The wing sections, flap
  effectiveness (except where calibrated), tail sizes and drag build-up are
  not the types' own. Stall characteristics, pitch-up and handling
  qualities are the Top G's.
- **No type performance data beyond the targets.** No take-off field length
  or climb figure is compared yet. The Phenom's published 3,209 ft take-off
  at MTOW sea-level ISA is the next one to add.
- **Thrust reversers** (Learjet 45, CRJ700, 737-800) use the core's generic
  cascade schedule and effectiveness.
